1. Executive Summary

You already have the pages this report would normally tell you to build. "Residential Interior Design" and "Commercial Interior Design" both exist, both are titled exactly right, and both are in your sitemap. The problem is that nothing connects them. Your front door at makenestinteriors.com is a Squarespace cover page — a beautiful full-screen illustration containing zero links, no heading, and the page title "MakeNest". Your real homepage lives at /home, and even that links to neither service page. So the two pages most likely to win you work are reachable only by typing their address. Meanwhile five of your ten main pages carry no meta description at all, /about and /process share an identical title and description, and your sitemap submits 32 individual client project pages to Google along with one called "delete" and two client-hub templates. None of this is a content problem — your content quality scores higher than the competitor beating you on every traffic metric. It is a wiring problem, and wiring is the cheap kind to fix.

2.4 Recommended Fix Priority

Link Residential and Commercial from your homepage

These two pages are already written and already titled correctly. Right now the only way to reach them is to know the address. Adding them to your main navigation is the single highest-return change available to you, and it takes minutes.

Give the front door somewhere to go

The cover page has no links at all. Either add navigation to it, or make /home your homepage so arrivals land on the page that has 14 links instead of the page that has none.

Write meta descriptions for the five pages missing them

/home, /residential, /commercial, /getstarted and /press. Both service pages and your enquiry page are currently letting Google invent their search-result text. 140–160 characters each.

Decide what to do about the 32 client project pages in your sitemap

Pages named for individual clients are publicly reachable and submitted to Google. If that is intentional, nothing to do. If it is not, they need a noindex and removal from the sitemap — and /client-hub-template and /delete should come out regardless.

Split the duplicated /about and /process titles

They currently share one title and one description. Give About a title about who you are, and Process a title about how you work.

Add the missing LocalBusiness fields

State, service area, coordinates and phone number. The block already exists — it just has holes in the fields local search uses.

Point /contact at /getstarted

A redirect so the address people guess first reaches your enquiry page instead of an error.

4.3 Strengths / Weaknesses

StrengthsThe material is genuinely good and the numbers agree: the highest content-quality score of all six sites benchmarked — above the competitor outranking you, and above all three of the aspirational designers you named. Accessibility, Best Practices and SEO all scored 100 in live Lighthouse runs on both mobile and desktop — rare, and evidence of a well-built site. The blog earns real positions, first and second for terms like "small luxury homes" and "nice homes". You already have correctly-titled Residential and Commercial service pages, which is the part most firms never get right. And your editorial link profile is better than the size of the practice would predict: a .edu from UVA's Blandy Experimental Farm, two Home & Design features including a profile of Paul, Northern Virginia Magazine, and the Preservation of Historic Winchester house tour.
WeaknessesAlmost everything working against you is a connection that was never made. The front door is a cover page with no links; the real homepage sits at a second URL and links to neither service page; five of ten pages have no meta description; /about and /process share one title and one description; /contact does not exist. The sitemap carries 200 URLs, of which 32 are individual client project pages plus a template and a page called "delete". The LocalBusiness markup is missing the state, service area and coordinates. One thing to be aware of but not alarmed by: most of your recent inbound links are auto-generated scraper spam — the same networks appear on all five competitors, they hit every small business site indiscriminately, and the only response needed is to not buy links.

7. Methodology Notes

  • Data gathered: July 30, 2026. Keyword volumes, domain metrics, backlink data, page audit and PageSpeed runs all captured the same day.
  • Site audit: the site is script-rendered, so every page figure was read from live page source in a real browser rather than from a simple crawler. Ten pages were audited in full and the complete 200-URL sitemap was read and classified.
  • Keyword wordings: every service term was searched three ways — with city and state, with city alone, and with no location. Rows shown as "No data" returned no volume record at all. That is deliberately distinguished from a measured zero, which is shown as 0.
  • Competitor selection: both intake-supplied competitors failed validation (one adjacent vertical, one in the wrong geography with no organic presence) and the third slot was a duplicate. All three competitors were sourced from live search results for the terms this business should rank for. Automated competitor suggestions were reviewed and set aside — they surfaced drapery and window-treatment retailers matching the blog's content rather than the practice's services.
  • Comparison basis: all eight competitive metrics come from a single source across all six domains so the columns are like-for-like. Thirty-day backlink figures were counted directly from the link index sorted by first-seen date, because the date filter returned zero for domains that demonstrably had links in the window — a tool fault, not a finding. The three aspirational competitors you supplied on August 3 were measured August 11; the original columns were measured July 30. Domain Rating shows a dash where the rating index has not yet computed a value for a newly-added domain — unmeasured is reported as unmeasured, never as zero.
  • Page speed: live Lighthouse runs on both mobile and desktop. Where a metric errored it is reported as not measured, never as a zero.
