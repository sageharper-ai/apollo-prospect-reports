1. Executive Summary

The headline finding is an opportunity hiding under the wrong keyword. The term supplied at intake — "financial advisor for women" — has essentially no measurable paid-search volume, so a campaign built on it would spend into a vacuum. But the data surfaced a clear, on-brand alternative right next to it: "financial advisor for divorce" draws about 5,400 searches a month at a PPC Difficulty of just 2 — meaning real demand with almost no advertisers competing for it. Paired with "certified divorce financial analyst" (1,900/mo) and "divorce financial advisor" (590/mo), the divorce-financial niche is the winnable ground: strong volume, low competition, moderate CPCs ($7–$12), and a precise match to the divorce-focused content and Divorce Prep Checklist you already publish. Before any of this can be built, though, one strategic decision has to be made — because PurseStrings is a two-sided platform (women use the directory free; advisors pay $996/yr to be listed), and a campaign aimed at growing consumer traffic looks completely different from one aimed at acquiring paying advisors. Both angles are laid out below with the data behind each. And because this is a YMYL, financial-and-legal-adjacent category, ad copy carries SEC/FINRA and Google Ads policy sensitivities that must be respected. Two items are open before build: the monthly budget (currently unset — and in a high-CPC vertical that materially shapes what is realistic) and the campaign-intent decision itself.

★ Decision needed before any campaign is built

PurseStrings makes money on the advisor side ($996/yr memberships) while women use the directory for free — so the pivotal question is: is this campaign meant to grow consumer directory traffic, or to acquire paying advisors? The two goals point to different keywords, different landing pages, and different budgets. This must be answered before a campaign is built.

Angle A — Grow consumer traffic (women) — The supplied keywords and the strong divorce-niche data fit here. Land paid traffic on a lead-magnet capture (the Divorce Prep Checklist), not the raw directory, so clicks become emails. Caveat: there is no direct revenue per consumer click — the return is indirect (more women make the directory more valuable to advisors).

Angle B — Acquire paying advisors ($996/yr) — Clear per-conversion value and a conversion-ready landing page already exists (/pro/, with a booking CTA). But the paid-search data is weak: advisor terms are either very expensive ("financial advisor lead generation" at $45/click) or have no measurable volume. Advisor acquisition looks better suited to content and outbound than to paid search.

What the data suggests: The keyword data favors Angle A on a divorce-niche footing — real volume, near-zero competition, on-brand content, and existing lead-magnet forms to convert against — while advisor acquisition via paid search looks expensive and thin. But this is a business-model decision, not just a data one, and it is yours to make; the report is built to inform it, not pre-empt it.

3.2 Value-Prop Patterns to Borrow or Counter

Recurring angles across the set — the messages your ads should either match or deliberately differentiate against.

  • Free-to-consumer + advisors-pay is the dominant model in this space (Willow, Wealthtender, SmartAsset) — your "Purse Strings Approved™" fits the pattern; differentiate on the women's-market + divorce specificity.
  • Certification / trust marks are the recurring credibility hook ("Advisor for Women™ Certificate," "Certified Advisor Reviews™") — lean on "Purse Strings Approved™" as the equivalent signal.
  • The divorce/widow niche is where competitors publish their sharpest landing pages (Wealthtender especially) — and it is exactly where your data and content are strongest. Own it.
  • Lead-gen platforms (SmartAsset, WiserAdvisor) set a high CPC ceiling on advisor terms — reinforcing that advisor acquisition is better via content/outbound than paid search.
  • "Free" is a competitor weapon on the consumer side (Savvy Ladies, the directories) — your consumer ads should convert on a specific lead magnet (the Divorce Prep Checklist), not compete on being free.
What is flagged as owed here. The value-prop angles above are read from competitors' public websites and the live organic search results — NOT from their live paid ad copy. Free tooling does not expose Google's Sponsored slots or the Ads Transparency Center, so we did not observe (and did not invent) the exact headlines Willow, Wealthtender, or SmartAsset are running. Given the presence of well-funded lead-gen platforms and RIAs, paid competition is near-certain — but a live paid-ad-copy / Ads-Transparency pull is owed as a follow-on to confirm who is bidding and with what messaging.

4. Audience + Targeting

Who to target, and how the two campaign angles split the audience — the concrete side of the strategic question above.

4.1 Demographic & Intent Signals

For the consumer (Angle A) divorce niche, the audience is women roughly age 40–60 navigating or anticipating divorce — high emotional and financial stakes, actively researching, and receptive to a trusted, specialist resource and a practical checklist. Layer in-market segments for financial planning and life-event (divorce) signals where the platform allows. For the advisor side (Angle B), the audience is financial professionals (advisors, CPAs, attorneys) seeking to grow a female client base — a much smaller, B2B audience better reached through LinkedIn/content than broad search. The two audiences barely overlap, which is why the campaign-intent decision cannot be deferred.

4.2 Geo Overlay

National (all 50 states) — the directory serves the whole US, so there is no geo-fence to lean on the way a local business would. That makes keyword intent and audience layering do all the targeting work, and it raises the importance of tight negatives and a specific landing page: a national, high-CPC, YMYL campaign with loose keywords is the fastest way to burn a budget. Consider starting with the highest-intent divorce-niche terms nationally, then using performance data to concentrate spend on the states and segments that convert.

4.3 Audiences to Test

Concrete starting audiences — specific enough to build in the ad platform this week.

Test A — Divorce-financial intent (consumer)

"financial advisor for divorce" + "certified divorce financial analyst", Phrase match, national, routed to the Divorce Prep Checklist lead magnet. The data's strongest bet — start here if Angle A is chosen.

Test B — Women's financial planning (consumer)

"financial planner for women" + on-brand supporting terms, layered with financial-planning in-market signals. Lower volume; a supporting group, not the anchor.

Test C — Advisor acquisition (advisor, if Angle B)

Advisor terms routed to /pro/ (already conversion-ready with a booking CTA). Expect high CPCs and thin volume — run small and measure hard, or shift this budget to content/LinkedIn outbound.

Ad-copy compliance note. This is a YMYL, financial-and-legal-adjacent category. Ad copy must avoid misleading or absolute claims (no "guaranteed returns," no "best advisor" superlatives), and because the platform hosts advisor profiles and testimonials, advisor-side promotion touches SEC/FINRA marketing rules (including the SEC Marketing Rule on testimonials/endorsements). Confirm current Google Ads financial-services policy at build time and have ad copy reviewed for compliance — this report flags the risk; it is not a compliance ruling.

5. Next Steps

Concrete moves for this week — ordered, and mostly free or low-cost.

Decide the campaign intent — consumer (Angle A) or advisor (Angle B)

This is the gating decision. The data favors a consumer, divorce-niche campaign (real volume, near-zero competition, on-brand content), but the choice is a business-model call only you can make. Everything downstream — keywords, landing page, budget — follows from it. Resolve this first.

Set a monthly budget and a target cost-per-lead

Budget is currently unset. In a national, YMYL, high-CPC vertical, a token budget underperforms — pick a monthly figure and a target cost per lead so scope and expectations are grounded. Even the low-competition divorce terms sit at $7–$12/click, so the budget directly sets how many leads are realistic.

If Angle A: launch the divorce niche into a lead-magnet page, with compliant copy

Point "financial advisor for divorce" and "certified divorce financial analyst" at a dedicated capture page built around the Divorce Prep Checklist (not the raw directory), confirm conversion tracking on the lead-capture form, and have the ad copy reviewed against Google Ads financial-services policy and SEC/FINRA testimonial rules before it goes live.

Methodology Notes

  • Data gathered: July 5, 2026. Both campaign angles are presented because the campaign-intent decision (consumer vs advisor) was open at research time.
  • Keyword data: pulled from a national US paid-search keyword database (this is a national, all-50-states platform — no local filter applies), then read for intent and on-brand fit.

  • Competitors: validated from their public websites and the live search results; live paid ad copy was NOT observed via free tooling and is flagged as owed — no ad copy was invented.
